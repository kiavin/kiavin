# The black hole in the header

The header is inspired by Gargantua in *Interstellar*. Its disk image is generated by a small numerical Kerr ray tracer; it is not a frame from the film. The green skill labels are an animated metaphor for curiosity and learning.

## Cinematic lettering

The title uses original polygon outlines for the six letters of **KELVIN**. A nonlinear deformation stretches the right-hand contours toward the disk and compresses them vertically. A slow 20-second cycle increases and releases the distortion. Outline echoes, engraved surface lines, luminous filaments, and travelling fragments connect the lettering to the accretion scene. The left side remains readable throughout the cycle.

The title deformation, echoes, and anamorphic flare are cinematic art direction, separate from the numerical black-hole model below. They are not a simulation of a physical sign, its material breakup, or its lensed images. Gravitational lensing itself does not disperse light into colours. Reduced-motion mode retains a deliberately distorted still composition and removes the title and particle motion.

## Geometry and light

The model uses an uncharged rotating black hole with dimensionless spin **a = 0.998**, viewed **82° from its spin axis**, close to edge-on. Distances use gravitational radii, `r_g = GM/c²`. The outer horizon is at about `1.0632 r_g`; the prograde innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) is about `1.2370 r_g`.

Each image sample traces a past-directed null geodesic from a distant observer through the Kerr exterior. The integration uses the separated radial and polar potentials, evolved with fourth-order Runge–Kutta in Mino time. Inverse radius and `cos(theta)` avoid the coordinate problems at radial infinity and the polar axis. Rays stop at the first intersection with the opaque equatorial disk, at capture, or on escape.

This produces the direct foreground disk and its distorted images above and below the dark region. Those arcs are different views of the same disk. The dark region is an apparent shadow, not a drawing of the event horizon. Higher-order disk images are included where the finite image resolution resolves them; no decorative photon ring is added.

The gas follows prograde circular Kerr orbits outside the ISCO. The photon frequency shift is `g = 1 / [u^t (1 − Ω lambda)]`, combining orbital motion and gravitational redshift. A sampled thermal spectrum evaluated at `g × T` accounts for the corresponding change in specific intensity. The approaching side consequently appears brighter and bluer; the receding side is dimmer and warmer.

## What is approximate

- The spacetime and photon equations are relativistic; integration and surface crossings are numerical approximations. The observer uses a distant-observer screen approximation at `10,000 r_g`.
- The disk is geometrically thin, opaque, and extends from the prograde ISCO to `20 r_g`. Its temperature follows a zero-torque Newtonian thin-disk profile, normalized to a peak emitted temperature of 14,000 K. This is an illustrative emissivity prescription, **not** a full Novikov–Thorne or GRMHD disk model.
- RGB uses three visible Planck samples rather than a complete colour-matching integral. Exposure, display gamma, slight bloom, and modest procedural texture are presentation choices. The picture is not calibrated observational data.
- Skill labels follow designed shrinking spirals, not timelike geodesics or a viscous fluid simulation. Their shrinking, warming, and fading are illustrative. An outside observer cannot see material emerge from inside the horizon. Motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences.
- This is not an exact reconstruction of Gargantua's mass, spin, accretion rate, or the film's artistic decisions.

## Reproduce

With Python, NumPy, SciPy and Pillow installed, run from the repository root:

```sh
python3 tools/render_black_hole.py --width 1080
python3 tools/build_header.py
python3 tools/check_physics.py
```

The standalone SVG embeds the rendered PNG and a visibility mask so it can display as one GitHub image. The mask prevents title fragments, labels and their guides from appearing in the captured-ray shadow. Letter outlines, skill labels and typography remain vector elements. The title builder uses `tools/cinematic_type.py` without an external font dependency. The JSON next to the PNG records render parameters and numerical residuals.

## Numerical checks

- The non-rotating limit returns `ISCO = 6 r_g`. Test rays on either side of the Schwarzschild critical impact parameter, `3 sqrt(3) r_g`, correctly separate capture and escape, with left/right symmetry.
- A 91 × 61 sample grid gives the same capture/disk/escape outcomes at Mino-time steps of `0.002` and `0.001`. Among rays hitting the disk at both resolutions, the 99th-percentile relative radius difference is approximately `9.0 × 10⁻⁵`, and the azimuth difference is approximately `5.6 × 10⁻⁴` radians.
- The full 1080 × 760 render resolves all 820,800 rays. Its sampled, normalized radial and polar first-integral residuals stay below `1.4 × 10⁻⁸` and `1.0 × 10⁻⁸`, respectively. These are numerical consistency checks, not a claim of observational or full fluid-model accuracy.
